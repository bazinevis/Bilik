* Chapter 1: Axioms of Probability
** 1.1 Introduction

*** Random, Certain, and Impossible Events

- *Random Event*: May or may not occur.
  - Example: A radioactive atom may or may not decay during a given time interval.
  - Example: An inspector may or may not find a defect in a product.

- *Certain Event*: Guaranteed to occur.
  - Example: Lightning is observed before thunder is heard.

- *Impossible Event*: Can never occur.
  - Example: An object traveling faster than the speed of light.

*** Why Probability Is Needed

Knowing that an event is random is not enough. We want to measure *how likely* it is to occur.

Observation:
- When an experiment is repeated many times, the proportion of occurrences of an event often approaches a constant value.
- Probability is used to describe this long-run behavior.

Example:
- Repeatedly tossing a fair coin results in the proportion of heads approaching 1/2.
- Therefore, P(Heads) = 1/2.

*** Historical Background

Probability originated from the study of games of chance and gambling.

Important contributors:
- Luca Pacioli (1445–1514)
- Niccolò Tartaglia (1499–1557)
- Girolamo Cardano (1501–1576)

- Pascal and Fermat exchanged letters in 1654 discussing methods for calculating probabilities.
- Christiaan Huygens published the first probability book in 1657:

*** Relative Frequency Interpretation

Probability was often defined as:

#+begin_quote
p = lim_{n→∞} n(A)/n
#+end_quote

where:
- n(A) = number of times event A occurs
- n = number of repetitions of the experiment

Interpretation:
- Probability is the limiting proportion of times an event occurs in repeated experiments.

*** Problems with the Frequency Definition

1. Infinite experiments cannot be performed in practice.
   - The limit cannot be computed exactly.
   - Approximation errors cannot be rigorously analyzed.

2. The limit may not exist.
   - Different sequences of experiments may produce different limiting values.
   - Uniqueness of probability is not guaranteed.

3. Subjective probabilities cannot be justified.
   Examples:
   - Probability that oil prices will rise in the next six months.
   - Probability that it will snow next Christmas.
   - Probability that Mozart was poisoned by Salieri.

*** Hilbert's Challenge

In 1900, David Hilbert presented 23 important mathematical problems.

One of them:
- Establish a rigorous axiomatic foundation for probability theory.

*** Kolmogorov's Axiomatic Approach (1933)

Andrei Kolmogorov successfully established the modern foundation of probability.

Key idea:
- Begin with a small set of self-evident axioms.
- Derive all probability theory logically from these axioms.

Advantages:
- Provides mathematical rigor.
- Avoids the problems of the frequency definition.
- Supports both frequentist and subjective interpretations of probability.

*** Main Takeaway

Probability was developed to quantify uncertainty.

Modern probability theory is based on Kolmogorov's axiomatic framework rather than solely on long-run frequencies. This framework provides a rigorous mathematical foundation for studying random events.
